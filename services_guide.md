# Greater Cincinnati Community Services Guide

## Purpose

This guide helps people identify community services in the Greater Cincinnati region based on their needs. It is designed for use in an AI resource navigator.

## Service Area

This guide is intended for people in the Greater Cincinnati region, including parts of Southwest Ohio and Northern Kentucky. United Way 211 of Greater Cincinnati serves Adams, Brown, Butler, Clermont, Clinton, Hamilton, and Warren counties in Ohio, and Boone, Campbell, Grant, and Kenton counties in Kentucky.

## 1. United Way 211 Greater Cincinnati

**Who it is for:** Anyone who is not sure where to start or needs help finding local services.

**When to use it:** When a person needs help with food, housing, utilities, transportation, mental health, employment, child care, legal services, or other community support.

**Services offered:** Resource navigation and referrals across a large database of local programs and agencies.

**How to start:** Dial **211** or call **(513) 721-7900** or **1-800-233-4357**.

**Best first step:** Start here if the person is unsure which organization is the best fit.

**Limitations:** 211 connects people to services but does not directly provide all services itself.

## 2. Food Assistance

**Who it is for:** People who need groceries, food pantries, community meals, or help finding food support.

**When to use it:** When someone is experiencing food insecurity or needs immediate food resources.

**Services offered:** Community meal listings, food pantry listings, and food-related referrals through local agencies and guides.

**Examples of resources mentioned in regional guides:** Freestore Foodbank, The Caring Place of Cincinnati, St. Francis Seraph Ministries, Queen City Kitchen, LIFE Food Pantry, and The Healing Center.

**How to start:** Contact **211** for the best current match or use the public library community resources guide for local meal and pantry listings.

**Best first step:** Ask whether the person needs groceries, prepared meals, or both.

**Limitations:** Hours and eligibility may vary by provider and may change over time.

## 3. Housing and Shelter Support

**Who it is for:** People experiencing homelessness, at risk of eviction, in need of emergency shelter, or looking for rent or housing support.

**When to use it:** When a person needs shelter placement, rent assistance, tenant rights information, or housing referrals.

**Services offered:** Emergency shelter access, housing referrals, public housing information, rent assistance, tenant rights support, and homelessness-related services.

**Examples of resources mentioned in regional guides:** Central Access Point (CAP), Cincinnati Metropolitan Housing Authority, Strategies to End Homelessness, Housing Opportunities Made Equal (HOME), Bethany House, Shelterhouse, and City Gospel Mission.

**How to start:** For shelter referral in Hamilton County, call **CAP: (513) 381-SAFE (7233)**. For general housing help, start with **211**.

**Best first step:** Determine whether the need is emergency shelter, rent help, housing rights, or longer-term housing support.

**Limitations:** Some housing and shelter programs have eligibility requirements or intake processes.

## 4. Mental Health and Crisis Support

**Who it is for:** People dealing with stress, emotional distress, mental health concerns, substance use concerns, or crisis situations.

**When to use it:** When a person needs counseling referrals, support groups, behavioral health resources, or crisis support.

**Services offered:** Mental health referrals through 211, crisis support lines, counseling resources, and behavioral health program referrals.

**Examples of resources mentioned in regional guides:** 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, Crisis Hotline at **(513) 281-CARE (2273)**, Central Clinic, NAMI Southwest Ohio, and addiction support providers.

**How to start:** Use **988** for suicide or crisis support. Use **911** for immediate emergency danger. For non-emergency navigation, start with **211**.

**Best first step:** Determine whether the issue is urgent, ongoing, or needs local counseling/support referral.

**Limitations:** In emergencies, do not rely only on a directory or chatbot. Use emergency services or crisis lines immediately.

## 5. Utility Assistance

**Who it is for:** People struggling to pay for electric, gas, heat, water, sewer, or telecommunications bills.

**When to use it:** When a person is behind on utility bills, at risk of shutoff, or needs help understanding assistance options.

**Services offered:** Utility assistance referrals through 211 and local agencies.

**Examples of resources mentioned in regional guides:** Community Action Agency, Duke Energy assistance resources, Salvation Army Heat Share, MEAC, and other local assistance programs.

**How to start:** Start with **211** for local options.

**Best first step:** Ask whether the person has a shutoff notice, which utility is affected, and where they live.

**Limitations:** Eligibility, documentation, and funding availability may vary.

## 6. Employment and Job Support

**Who it is for:** People looking for jobs, training, career support, or employment resources.

**When to use it:** When someone needs job readiness help, career counseling, or employment referrals.

**Services offered:** Job training, career fairs, employment agencies, continuing education, and career counseling resources.

**How to start:** Contact **211** for referrals to current employment services.

**Best first step:** Clarify whether the person needs a job now, resume help, training, or long-term career support.

**Limitations:** Not every service provides direct placement.

## 7. Family, Youth, and Child Services

**Who it is for:** Families needing child-related support, school support, baby supplies, or family-oriented services.

**When to use it:** When a family needs help with youth services, school-related barriers, childcare navigation, or family support programs.

**Services offered:** Referrals to youth and family services, baby supplies, childcare-related resources, and school-connected supports.

**Examples of resources mentioned in regional guides:** Project Connect, baby supplies through 211 categories, and local family support resources.

**How to start:** Start with **211** for broad family support.

**Best first step:** Ask the age of the child, the main concern, and whether the issue affects housing, school, food, or childcare.

**Limitations:** Some services are tied to county, school district, or family eligibility rules.

## 8. Transportation Support

**Who it is for:** People who need help getting to appointments, services, or essential activities.

**When to use it:** When transportation is a barrier to health care, social services, work, or other basic needs.

**Services offered:** Transportation-related referrals through 211 and some specialized community programs.

**Examples of resources mentioned in regional guides:** Health-related transportation support and senior support programs such as Meals on Wheels Southwest OH & Northern KY.

**How to start:** Start with **211** to identify the right transportation resource.

**Best first step:** Ask what kind of ride is needed, where the person lives, and whether the trip is for health care, work, food, or another essential need.

**Limitations:** Transportation support may be limited by geography, eligibility, or purpose of trip.

## 9. Legal and Advocacy Services

**Who it is for:** People with housing rights issues, low-income legal questions, discrimination concerns, or advocacy needs.

**When to use it:** When someone needs advice about eviction, unsafe housing, discrimination, or related civil legal issues.

**Services offered:** Legal referrals and advocacy resources through regional organizations.

**Examples of resources mentioned in regional guides:** Legal Aid Society of Greater Cincinnati and Housing Opportunities Made Equal (HOME).

**How to start:** For general navigation, start with **211**. If the issue involves housing rights or civil legal help, look to Legal Aid or HOME as appropriate.

**Best first step:** Clarify whether the issue is eviction, discrimination, unsafe housing, benefits, or another civil legal problem.

**Limitations:** Legal services often depend on eligibility, issue type, and capacity.

## 10. Disability and Accessibility Services

**Who it is for:** People with disabilities, vision or hearing needs, developmental disabilities, or accessibility support needs.

**When to use it:** When someone needs independent living support, disability services, accessibility assistance, or related referrals.

**Services offered:** Disability support programs, independent living services, and accessibility-related organizations.

**Examples of resources mentioned in regional guides:** Independence Alliance, CABVI, Hamilton County DD Services, Hearing Speech and Deaf Center, and LADD.

**How to start:** Use **211** for navigation, or contact a specialized organization if the need is clear.

**Best first step:** Ask what kind of support is needed and whether the person needs immediate services, accommodations, advocacy, or connection to ongoing support.

**Limitations:** Some services are specialized and may require documentation or county-based eligibility.

## General Guidance Rules

- If a person is not sure where to start, recommend **United Way 211 Greater Cincinnati** first.
- If the need is urgent and involves immediate danger, instruct the person to call **911**.
- If the need involves suicide, severe emotional distress, or a mental health crisis, direct the person to **988** or other crisis support.
- If the need is shelter placement in Hamilton County, consider **CAP** as a key starting point.
- If more than one resource fits, provide the top one or two options and explain why.
- Do not invent hours, eligibility rules, addresses, contacts, or service details that are not included in the knowledge source.
- Remind users that service hours, funding, and availability can change.
